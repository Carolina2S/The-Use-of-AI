Long exposure

A tutorial about slowing time down. A long exposure only works if you decide what stays still and what moves. No AI needed: just a tripod, patience and an eye.

Tutorial

  1. Pick something that movesWater, clouds, car lights, people. Include one still element, a rock or a building, to anchor the frame.
  2. Lock the camera downTripod on solid ground. Turn off lens stabilisation, since it fights a tripod.
  3. Set it to manualLowest ISO (100), aperture around f/8 to f/11, shoot RAW. Focus first, then switch to manual focus so it doesn't hunt.
  4. Fire without touching the cameraUse a 2-second timer or a remote. Even a finger press blurs the whole frame.
  5. In daylight, add an ND filterIt darkens the scene so the shutter can stay open. Each stop doubles the exposure time. Past 30 seconds, switch to Bulb.
